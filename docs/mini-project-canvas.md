|          MOTIVATION  🎯 *Which is the target group of our mini-project? Who is the end-user? Car drivers in central Helsinki without a residential parking permit, especially visitors and occasional drivers unfamiliar with local parking rules. What are their objectives? What needs do we need to address with our work? Street parking legality is hard to judge on the spot. The rule depends on the day, the hour and the street side, and the governing sign may be out of sight. Drivers need to know whether they may park in the area they chose, whether it costs money, and until when.How will they benefit from this proposed solution?  Drivers avoid fines and unexpected charges, and know when they must move the car. Helsinki recorded 156,383 parking fines and 9,341 warnings in 2023 (City of Helsinki, Pysäköintivirheet Helsingissä, Helsinki Region Infoshare, CC BY 4.0). Of the 8,754 street parking areas the register states a complete rule for 7,391; 978 lack hours and 385 contradict themselves or cannot be parsed. The app says which of the three it is, every time.* |  DATA COLLECTION  🧩 *Which data sources are we planning to use? | **LEARNING TASK  🐭(focus on problem definition)** *Define the problem setting.**978 of Helsinki's 8,754 street parking areas have a known rule type but no stated hours, so the app cannot tell drivers when parking is paid or restricted there. We predict these missing hours from nearby parking areas of the same type, and show "unknown" where no close neighbour exists.** Is this supervised / unsupervised / other…?**Supervised. The 5,823 parking areas whose hours the register does state serve as labelled examples.**Classification / regression / other…?**Classification. Each hour pattern, such as weekdays 9-21 and Saturdays 9-18, is one class.**What are we planning to learn? E.g. What is the target variable/learning outcome?**The hours pattern of a parking area.**What variables are we using as input?**Hours of the nearest parking areas of the same type, distance to them, whether they are on the same street, parking class and district.***  | **LEARNING APPROACH  🪤(focus on solution implementation)** *Which ML/statistical methods seem more relevant for the defined problem setting and why?* ***K-nearest neighbours restricted to parking areas of the same class. We measured this before choosing it: hiding the hours of every area that has them and copying from the nearest same-class area is correct 97.5% of the time, against 32.8% for a dummy classifier that always guesses the most common pattern.** Which evaluation metrics could be relevant?Accuracy and macro-F1, since some hour patterns are rare, both against the dummy baseline. Coverage: the share of the 978 areas that get a prediction at all. The distance cut-off comes from the accuracy-versus-coverage trade-off, favouring accuracy, because a wrong answer can cost a fine while "unknown" costs nothing.Is any special treatment relevant regarding how we choose to split the data or how we cross-validate?Yes. Neighbouring sections of one street are near-duplicates, so a random split would test on copies of the training data and inflate the score. We use spatially blocked cross-validation, holding out whole districts. Areas lacking hours also sit far from known ones, a median of 153 m with only 17.7% within 50 m, so we also test with close neighbours hidden, to match the distances we will really face.* |  | **COMMUNICATION OF RESULTS  📢** *Which type of deliverable will benefit most the end-user? Predicted hours need a nearby area of the same class, and those are dense in the centre and sparse outside it, so drivers in outer districts will see "unknown" more often. We report coverage per district rather than one city-wide average, so the gap is visible instead of hidden.**Is there a need to ask for consent during the data collection process?**No. Every source describes street infrastructure, not people, and all of it is already published openly under CC BY 4.0.**Is there a need for data pseudonymization/anonymization?**No, because no personal data enters the pipeline. The driver's location is read on their own device and never sent anywhere.**Any other privacy considerations that come to mind?**The app must never claim to grant permission. It shows the city's rule and where it came from, and says plainly when it does not know, because a confident wrong answer costs the driver a fine.*** |
